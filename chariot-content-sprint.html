Speculative portfolio project

Make invisible DAF friction visible.

A compact content sprint showing how I would turn Chariot’s technical product knowledge into useful stories across blog, social, email, video, and sales.

Goal
Build trust by making a niche financial workflow easier to understand.

Audience
Nonprofit fundraising, development operations, and finance teams.
01 / The insight

The donation is approved. The hard part can still be figuring out what arrived.

THE FRICTION

DAF reconciliation can become detective work.

A DAF gift may arrive after the donor made the decision to give, and matching the incoming payment to the right donor and purpose can create operational friction.

THE STORY

This is not just a finance problem.

Slow or unclear reconciliation can affect donor acknowledgement, stewardship, reporting, and the overall giving experience.

THE CONTENT OPPORTUNITY

Explain the workflow in plain English.

Start with a problem a fundraiser recognizes, then show how better payment data and tracking can reduce the manual work behind the gift.

This concept is based on publicly available Chariot materials. Chariot documents that DAF reconciliation can be challenging because nonprofits may receive grant payments after the donor’s decision, with limited visibility into the original gift details.
02 / Anchor content

One useful idea, built to travel.

Blog / Guide
Your DAF gift arrived. Now whose gift is it?
Why reconciliation becomes a donor-experience problem, not just a back-office problem.
A donor can decide to support your organization in a few minutes. The operational trail that follows may take much longer. When a DAF grant arrives without the context your team needs, someone has to connect the payment to the donor, the campaign, and the intended purpose before stewardship can really begin.

That gap matters. The faster your team can identify a gift, the faster you can thank the donor, update your records, and keep the relationship moving. DAF reconciliation is not only about closing the books. It is part of the donor experience.

06 / Why this approach

Start with the customer problem. Earn the right to talk about the product.

SPECIFIC

Lead with a recognizable pain.

“Whose gift is this?” is more useful than generic education about DAFs.

REUSABLE

Build once, distribute intelligently.

One strong insight can become editorial, social, email, video, and sales content without feeling copied and pasted.

MEASURABLE

Every asset has a job.

Measure whether the content earns attention, creates intent, and becomes useful to sales, not simply whether it shipped.
